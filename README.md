
## Customer Repeat Purchase Behaviour and Its Potential Drivers

### 1. What Is Repeat Purchase Behaviour?

Repeat purchase behaviour occurs when a customer makes purchases on multiple occasions rather than buying only once. In this project, customer transaction history is used to study repeat purchasing patterns and identify behaviours associated with future purchases.

### 2. Factors That May Influence Repeat Purchases

The dataset can help investigate the following potential drivers:

- **Purchase frequency:** Customers with more previous invoices may have established shopping habits.
- **Product variety:** Customers purchasing different products may have broader needs and more reasons to return.
- **Total spending:** Higher spending may indicate stronger engagement, although spending alone does not prove loyalty.
- **Recency:** The time since a customer's latest purchase may help indicate whether they are still actively shopping.
- **Average basket value:** The typical value of an order may reveal differences between customer purchasing patterns.
- **Time between orders:** Shorter gaps between invoices may indicate more frequent shopping.
- **Product availability and suitability:** Customers may return when products meet their needs and remain available.
- **Customer experience:** Service quality, delivery, pricing and satisfaction could affect repeat purchases, but these factors are not directly measured in the available transaction fields.

### 3. What Can Be Concluded From This Dataset?

The transaction data can reveal associations between purchasing history and repeat-purchase outcomes. However, it cannot independently establish the exact reason why a customer returned. For example, satisfaction, advertising exposure, delivery experience and price sensitivity should not be presented as proven causes unless additional evidence is available.

The machine-learning target `repeat_purchase_next_90d` is designed to predict whether a customer purchases again during the specified future prediction window. The model's predictive performance does not, by itself, establish causation.

### 4. Business Recommendations

1. Identify customers with a history of frequent purchases and offer relevant product recommendations.
2. Monitor customers whose usual purchase intervals have become longer.
3. Recommend complementary products based on previous purchases.
4. Test suitable, ethical loyalty offers and measure whether they increase repeat purchasing.
5. Track repeat-purchase rates by customer cohort and product category.
6. Evaluate campaigns using controlled comparisons where practical.

### 5. Conclusion

Repeat purchasing is an important indicator of customer retention. Historical transaction features can help identify customers who are more likely to purchase again. Businesses can use these findings to develop targeted retention strategies, but the underlying reasons for customer behaviour require further evidence and validation.
